About · Kartik Nand, founder

Software Fiji businesses can afford

I'm Kartik Nand. I started KaiTech to make software that a business in Fiji can afford, and that fits the way work is done here.

The problem

Most business software is built somewhere else, for somewhere else. It is priced for bigger markets. It assumes fast internet in every room and on every road. It has never heard of FNPF, of PAYE the way Fiji does it, or of a customer who pays by M-PAiSA.

So a business here pays a lot for a tool that fits badly, then fills the gaps with spreadsheets, paper and phone calls. The owner ends up doing the software's job.

Building it the other way round

KaiTech starts from the work, then writes the software.

  • KaiWorkforce works out FNPF and PAYE, because payroll here depends on them.
  • BillZilla takes M-PAiSA and MyCash next to bank and cash, and keeps invoice numbers in order with no gaps.
  • KaiInspect keeps saving an inspection when the phone has no signal, and reaches cleaners through a WhatsApp link, with no account to set up.
  • Drivo plans delivery runs on Fiji's own roads and runs on open-source maps, so there are no map fees to pass on.

What affordable means

You take the one product you need. Each works on its own, and they connect when you run more than one: confirmed invoices in BillZilla become delivery routes in Drivo.

The price is set for businesses here. Good software should not be something only the largest companies in the country can pay for.

Where it stands

Four products are live. KaiTech works with businesses including NAFCO and Totoka Pacific.
